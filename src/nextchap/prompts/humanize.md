VOICE AND HUMANITY RULES. The chapters already in this book are the authority. Where a rule
below conflicts with how those chapters are actually written, the book wins.

- Write the established voice: its sentence lengths, word level, punctuation habits and verbal
  tics. Never upgrade or simplify the vocabulary of the book.
- Vary sentence length hard. Follow a long, unfolding sentence with a short one. Fragments
  count. Never write three sentences in a row of similar length, and never let the middle of a
  scene settle into one comfortable band.
- Vary paragraph length too, and never close every paragraph on a beat. Some paragraphs should
  just stop. One-sentence paragraphs are right where the book uses them.
- Say the thing directly. No negation pivots ("not just X, it's Y", "not X but Y"), no
  significance inflation ("marked a pivotal moment", "stood as a testament to"), no hedge
  stacks ("a mix of", "seemed to", "somewhat"), no rhetorical question answered in the next
  breath, no speaking for the reader with an explanatory sentence after a beat has already
  landed, and no recap at the end of a scene or chapter.
- No stock machine phrasing: "turns out", "what he did not expect was", "little did he know",
  "the air was thick with", "a shiver ran down her spine", "only time would tell",
  "unbeknownst to them", "meticulously", "a symphony of", "labyrinth of", "furthermore",
  "moreover", "in that moment".
- No rhetorical scaffolding: no three-beat parallel lists, no repeated sentence openers, no
  either/or framing, no balanced pair of clauses that mirror each other, no aphorism to close
  a paragraph. Human prose is lopsided; machine prose is symmetric.
- Do not trade one crutch for another. Drop a stock phrase and the same replacement must not
  land everywhere in its place, and sentence after sentence must not open the same way. The fix
  for a cliche is never a different cliche.
- Let the grammar stay as irregular as the book is: fragments, sentences that open with And or
  But, a comma splice while the pace is fast, dropped subjects and interruptions in dialogue.
  Never typos, misspellings, wrong homophones, garbled word order, or drifted tense and POV.
- Do not over-correct into a different artificiality. A slightly awkward phrase or an informal
  word is human; prose visibly straining to look human is not.
- Keep the dialogue theirs. No character states their own subtext, and no two characters share
  one vocabulary.
